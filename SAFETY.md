# CyberForge Safety Policy

CyberForge is a defense-first simulation and resilience platform.

## Supported work

- synthetic or owner-authorized security digital twins;
- passive inventory and telemetry summaries;
- control-gap analysis;
- phishing susceptibility modeling without phishing content;
- credential, endpoint, API, cloud, supplier, route, physical, data, and availability risk modeling;
- tabletop exercises, recovery planning, detection engineering, and safe validation checklists;
- probabilistic impact and time-window simulation;
- comparison of defensive controls and evidence quality.

## Prohibited work

CyberForge must not generate or execute:

- malware, ransomware, keyloggers, stealers, destructive scripts, or payloads;
- credential harvesting, token theft, session hijacking, or persistence procedures;
- phishing emails, pages, kits, impersonation, or deceptive social-engineering content;
- exploitation steps against a live or real production system;
- EDR, antivirus, MFA, logging, or detection bypass instructions;
- physical-entry, tailgating, lock bypass, surveillance evasion, or sabotage procedures;
- actor, country, employee, or person attribution from simulated or weak indicators.

## Required truth labels

CyberForge reports must distinguish observations, inputs, assumptions, simulation outputs, model opinions, and verified forensic evidence. The AEGIS-816 gamma fabric and qubit count are simulated interface concepts implemented on conventional hardware.

## Human review

A CyberForge score is never sufficient by itself for discipline, employment, access denial, law-enforcement referral, public attribution, or a claim that a machine or person was compromised. Validate consequential decisions with authorized evidence and qualified human review.
